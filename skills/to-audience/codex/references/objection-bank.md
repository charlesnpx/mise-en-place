# Objection bank

Prompt questions for building the objection/FAQ section of any full-mode artifact. Answer the hardest ones *before* drafting the rest — they shape scope, framing, and the ask.

## Universal prompts

- Why now? What changed?
- What do we stop doing to make room for this?
- What breaks if adoption stalls at 30%?
- Why is this better than the status quo / the rival option / doing nothing?
- What is the cheapest version of this that still proves the point?
- What would make us kill this? Who decides, and when?
- Who bears the cost, and who gets the benefit? Are they the same people?
- What does this assume that could turn out false?
- What happened the last time something like this was tried here?

## Decision-type prompts

**Funding / approval asks**
- What is the total cost including the people we'd redirect?
- What do we get at the review date that we can't get today?
- What is the downside if it fails — money lost, time lost, or credibility lost?

**Adoption / behavior-change asks**
- What does the first week look like for the person being asked to change?
- What do they lose — autonomy, a tool they like, a workflow they've optimized?
- What happens to the people who opt out?

**Technical / architecture choices**
- What workload or edge case does this handle worst?
- What is the migration path, and what is the rollback path?
- Who maintains it in year two?

## Selecting and writing the objections

1. Generate candidates from the prompts above plus anything the audience has actually said.
2. Pick the 3–6 the audience would rank hardest — not the ones easiest to answer.
3. **Steel-man each one**: state it in the form its proponents would recognize. A weak version convinces no one and reads as evasion.
4. Refute, don't dismiss: "Some will argue **X**; that is not decisive because **Y**; the evidence is **Z**." Each Z must be reader-legible.
5. If an objection cannot be honestly refuted, concede it and show the mitigation or scope it out of the ask. An honest concession buys credibility for the rest.
